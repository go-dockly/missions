NORWEGIAN TELEMARK · FEBRUARY 1943

The historical foundation is extraordinary.

Vemork, near Rjukan in Telemark, housed a heavy-water production facility operated by Norsk Hydro. Heavy water was important to nuclear research, and the Allies feared German access to it could support an atomic weapons programme.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://snl.no\&sz=32)

Store norske leksikon+1



The actual operation, Gunnerside, took place on the night of 27–28 February 1943.

Nine Norwegian saboteurs ultimately took part. They crossed the gorge rather than use the guarded bridge, entered the plant, destroyed the heavy-water production equipment and escaped without a shot being fired or anyone being killed during the operation. Approximately 500 kg of heavy water was lost.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.nia.no\&sz=32)

NIA+2



That's a fascinating design challenge:

**How do we turn an operation that was historically almost flawlessly executed into an emotionally compelling tactical game mission without falsifying its history?**

My answer would be to make our fictional squad part of the wider resistance operation, rather than replacing the historical Gunnerside team.

**Mission premise**

Intelligence from London suggests that German-controlled heavy-water production is continuing at Vemork. A Norwegian resistance network has been tasked with supporting an operation to destroy the production capability.

Our squad is assigned a parallel support operation. The historical Gunnerside team carries out the actual sabotage; our characters must protect the wider resistance network and keep the escape operation from being compromised.

I'd split the mission into five acts.

### **Act I — The White Wilderness**

The player arrives on the Hardangervidda plateau.

This isn't a conventional infiltration mission yet.

It's about survival and preparation.

- Locate a missing resistance contact.
- Recover essential intelligence.
- Navigate extreme weather.
- Avoid German patrols.
- Establish communications with the local network.

The player gets an appreciation of how difficult the environment really is.

The weather should matter. Snow changes visibility, wind interferes with communications, and movement through difficult terrain becomes a tactical consideration.

### **Act II — The Industrial Ghost**

We move closer to Vemork.

The player observes the facility and surrounding infrastructure.

This is where our simulation systems shine:

- Guards change shifts.
- Workers arrive and leave.
- Security personnel inspect suspicious activity.
- Railway and industrial operations follow schedules.
- Radio communications reveal changes in enemy activity.

Rather than immediately attacking the installation, the player has to understand it.

The night of 27–28 February.

The historical Gunnerside operation is underway.

Our squad has its own simultaneous objectives:

- Protect a local resistance contact.
- Keep an alternative communications channel operational.
- Prevent a nearby German search party from compromising the escape.
- Secure a route for our own team.

We hear fragments of radio communications.

We see distant figures moving around the facility.

Then, just after midnight, the heavy-water installation is destroyed.

No grand battle. No massive firefight.

Just an explosion in the middle of the night.

And then the entire valley starts to wake up.

### **Act IV — The Hunt**

The Germans now understand that something serious has happened.

Patrols are mobilised. The roads are checked. Communication channels become unreliable.

The player must extract the squad while protecting the people who helped them.

This is where the mission turns from a carefully planned operation into an increasingly desperate escape.

### **Act V — The Last Transmission**

This is where I would bring Luc back into the story.

I would make this the emotional climax.

Luc is operating a secondary radio station, maintaining a false transmission to keep German search parties away from the actual escape route.

As the situation deteriorates, the Germans locate his position.

The player hears the radio conversation:

**Luc:** "Everyone out? Good. Then I've done my job."

**Commanding officer:** "Luc, get out of there. That's an order."

**Luc:** "You always were terrible at giving orders."

A pause.

**Luc:** "Tell the others I owe them a drink."

The transmission continues.

The player sees the search party approaching the cabin in the distance. Snow and wind surround the small building. The radio mast is illuminated by a searchlight.

Then the transmission breaks into static.

We don't need to show his body.

We don't even need to show the explosion.

The player simply knows.

And when the squad reunites later, there's one empty place.

I'd preserve the fact that the actual Gunnerside saboteurs escaped without casualties: Luc belongs to our fictional parallel team, not the historical nine.&#x20;

# **3. Don't end the Vemork storyline with the explosion**

This is something I feel strongly about.

The historical story didn't end with Gunnerside.

In fact, it became even more complicated.

[“Freshman” - War History](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/gIA8qW_FC6Yy0ojuscyx-n3i8hf7VYin4kwq_FRx3oREWs5sNArjGQXogGlwpMz63XPHdXBiByS7p83vznjZAZrZzpvE1Ki0gTonIm-fFpnlMjINamJkr42hk65Np5d-jT2hMd-rr9Vc38PKuSmEGuB2sIJWErWWvtBJsXPIa_I?purpose=inline)

[warhistory.org](https://warhistory.org/%40msw/article/freshman?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

Production at Vemork restarted within months. In November 1943, the Allies bombed the industrial area, causing civilian deaths. In February 1944, Norwegian saboteurs sank the ferry DF Hydro carrying heavy-water-related cargo. Eighteen people died, including fourteen Norwegian civilians.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.nia.no\&sz=32)

NIA+1



This gives us an opportunity for a much more mature narrative.

The squad eventually discovers that even successful military operations have consequences.

We could build an entire campaign arc around:

- The resistance operation.
- The German response.
- The Allied bombing decision.
- The civilian consequences.
- The subsequent ferry operation.

And force the characters to confront the difference between winning an operation and doing the right thing.

One historical nuance I'd preserve: Allied leaders genuinely feared a German nuclear breakthrough, but Germany was much further from a working reactor, let alone an atomic bomb, than popular accounts sometimes suggest. A September 2026 scientific study reinforces just how far short German resources fell.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.eurekalert.org\&sz=32)

EurekAlert!+1



That makes the story even more interesting: our characters act on intelligence available at the time, under uncertainty, with enormous consequences.

# **4. What this means for our game design**

I would make Vemork a showcase for several systems we've been discussing.

| System                   | Vemork implementation                                          |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Enemy AI                 | Industrial facility security and organised searches            |
| Perception               | Snow, darkness, industrial noise and lighting                  |
| Squad command            | Separate teams executing simultaneous objectives               |
| Environmental simulation | Machinery, electricity, weather and terrain                    |
| Intelligence             | Resistance contacts and intercepted transmissions              |
| Campaign                 | Historical consequences and persistent character relationships |
| Narrative                | Luc's death and its impact on the squad                        |

And I'd make the mission genuinely challenging without resorting to artificial difficulty.

The plateau should be just as dangerous as the German guards.
